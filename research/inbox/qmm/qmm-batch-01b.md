## 2. Places and layout
- **Academy Gu room and store:** Located in the academy. The Gu room contains Rank 1 Gu worms that students can pick for free initially, or pay for extras (ch 23). Beside it is a small store with a counter and chest, run by Jiang Ya, which sells grass puppets (ch 23).
- **Academy training field:** Paved with bluestone floor tiles. Features a bamboo wall where grass puppets are lined up for target practice (ch 24, 25).
- **Academy gateway:** The only route in and out of the school. A primary chokepoint where Fang Yuan blocks students to extort them (ch 26, 27). Near a pavilion where the elder and guards watch (ch 27).
- **Academy dormitory (hostel):** Built directly on the ground, making it humid with wet wooden doors (unlike typical wooden lodges built above ground on stilts) (ch 34). It has guards posted outside (ch 32). Inside, Fang Yuan's room has simple furniture and a bed (ch 32), with space underneath to store wine jars (ch 23, 29). 
- **Inn:** Has a first-floor dining room with window tables offering views of the streets and distant mountains (ch 31, 38). Sells meals and green bamboo wine (ch 23). 
- **Terraced fields:** Located halfway up Qing Mao Mountain. Resembles a green sea of wheat sprouts with water canals for irrigation. Worked entirely by mortal farmers (ch 39).
- **Merchant caravan camp:** Set up on the village boundaries by dusk. Composed of temporary red, blue, green, and yellow tents, squeezed together with small street stalls (ch 39).
- **Rock gambling den:** A large grey tent taking up about one mu of land (ch 40). The entrance features two pillars carved with couplets promising good fortune (ch 40). Inside, the atmosphere is quiet and carpeted (ch 40, 41). It has three rows of counters (left, right, middle) displaying amber and fossils, with larger rocks resting on the floor (ch 40). 
- **Mo family estate:** Contains a garden with a wooden training puppet (ch 31). Features a brightly lit study room with a desk where Mo Chen practices calligraphy (ch 31, 37). It has a busy front entrance and a secluded back door (ch 37).
- **Chi family branch home:** Contains a brightly lit study room (ch 29).
- **Uncle's home:** Features a hall with wide-back huge chairs (ch 30).
- **Qing Mao Mountain roads and surroundings:** Characterized by steep cliffs, falling rocks, deep ominous forests, and straight bamboo forests (ch 39). Features wild grass and wildflowers (ch 39). Roads are muddy during the rain (ch 38).
- **Bai Gu Mountain:** A nearby mountain containing a secretly built Rank 4 righteous path inheritance (ch 43).

## 3. Factions and power
- **Gu Yue clan:** Maintains authority over mortals, who are treated as cheap labor/servants (ch 38, 39). Clan rules strictly forbid using Gu to fight in the academy (ch 27, 34) and elders interfering in youth fights (ch 31, 37). Gu Masters wear identifying belts: Rank 1 wear blue belts with a copper "1" plate, Rank 2 wear red belts with a steel "2" plate (ch 31). Academy gives a weekly subsidy of 3 primeval stones (ch 23). 
- **Mo family branch:** A powerful faction led by elder Gu Yue Mo Chen (ch 31). His heirs are Mo Yan and Mo Bei (ch 31). They employ mortal servants like Gao Wan (ch 31). They feud with the Chi family and the clan head's faction (ch 32). Highly concerned with public reputation and avoiding scandals that show weakness (ch 37).
- **Chi family branch:** Led by elder Gu Yue Chi Lian, grandfather to Chi Cheng (ch 29). Feuds with the Mo family (ch 29). Must project strength to keep their political supporters loyal (ch 29). 
- **Clan head faction:** Led by Gu Yue Bo (ch 39). The academy elder favors this faction and protects Fang Zheng (ch 25).
- **Jia clan caravan:** Travels between mountains to trade, overcoming beasts and harsh terrain (ch 39). Led by Rank 4 Jia Fu (ch 39). They employ Rank 1 clerks wearing green belts (ch 40). They set up gambling dens and shops (ch 39, 40).

## 4. People
- **Gu Yue Fang Yuan:** Rank 1 initial stage, but uses the Liquor worm to produce middle-stage primeval essence (ch 29, 35). Extorts his entire class twice for primeval stones (ch 28, 30). Murders a servant in cold blood, chops up the corpse, and uses it to blackmail the Mo family (ch 35, 36). Gambles on rocks using his future knowledge to win a rare Gu (ch 42).
- **Jiang Ya:** Rank 1 Gu Master in his 20s. Runs the academy store. Arrogant toward mortals but friendly to Gu Masters (ch 23).
- **Martial arts instructor:** Rank 2 Gu Master. Has bronzed, scarred skin from his copper skin Gu. Pragmatic but teaches chaotically (ch 24).
- **Academy Elder:** Rank 3. Prefers to let students fight to build combat skills (ch 28). Shocked by Fang Yuan's ruthlessness but covers it up while trying to limit his influence (ch 36).
- **Gu Yue Mo Bei:** B-grade talent, Mo family heir. Uses a blunt fighting style. Knocked out twice by Fang Yuan during extortions (ch 24, 27, 31).
- **Gu Yue Chi Cheng:** C-grade talent cheating as a B-grade, Chi family heir. Short and pockmarked. Beaten up by Fang Yuan (ch 24, 27, 29).
- **Gu Yue Fang Zheng:** A-grade talent, Fang Yuan's twin. Humiliated when Fang Yuan extorts him along with everyone else (ch 25, 27). 
- **Gu Yue Mo Chen:** Mo family elder, Mo Bei's grandfather. Pragmatic and politically savvy. Realizes Fang Yuan's chopped corpse is a calculated threat and pays him off rather than escalate (ch 31, 37).
- **Gu Yue Mo Yan:** Rank 2 middle stage, Mo Bei's sister. Has a horse-face (ch 32). Tries to intimidate Fang Yuan but is outsmarted by his reliance on academy rules (ch 33). Grounded for 7 days by her grandfather (ch 37).
- **Gao Wan:** Mo family servant and mortal martial artist. Muscular with yellowish skin (ch 31). Blocks Fang Yuan's door, fights him physically, but is decapitated by Fang Yuan's moonblades (ch 34, 35).
- **Gu Yue Chi Lian:** Chi family elder. Tells Chi Cheng to fight his own battles to project a strong image (ch 29).
- **Gu Yue Dong Tu & Aunt:** Fang Yuan's uncle and aunt. Try to use the extortion incident to drive a wedge between Fang Zheng and Fang Yuan (ch 30).
- **Gu Yue Bo:** Rank 4 Clan Head. Greets the Jia clan caravan upon arrival (ch 39).
- **Jia Fu:** Rank 4, Jia clan caravan leader. Fat with a circular pockmarked face. Rides a giant toad (ch 39).
- **Jia Jin Sheng:** Rank 1, Jia Fu's half-brother. Runs the rock gambling den. Tries to cheat Fang Yuan but ends up buying his toad Gu for 500 stones (ch 42).

## 5. Gu and cultivation
- **Moonlight Gu:** Rank 1. Shoots curved blue blades up to 10 meters (ch 24, 25). Consumes 2 moon orchid petals per meal, twice a day (ch 23).
- **Liquor worm:** Consumes green bamboo wine, 1 jar lasting 4 days (ch 23). Refines primeval essence by condensing it up a minor realm (e.g., initial jade green to middle pale green) (ch 29, 43). 
- **Spring Autumn Cicada:** Consumes time directly from the River of Time (ch 23).
- **Scarecrow Gu:** Used to craft grass puppets (ch 23).
- **Copper skin Gu:** Hardens the user's skin to a bronzed color for physical defense (ch 24).
- **Treasure Brass Toad:** A 2.5-meter tall orange-yellow toad with bronze nail-like warts. Used as a beast of burden (ch 39).
- **Brute Force Longhorn Beetle Gu:** Gives the user the physical strength of a cow (ch 39).
- **Mudskin Toad:** Rank 2. Yellow body with boils and warts. Requires 500 grams of fertile yellow soil per meal. A base material for refining the Treasure Brass Toad. Worth 500 stones (ch 42).
- **Rules and Regulations Gu:** From the legend of Ren Zu. A cubic "Regulations" and spherical "Rules" Gu that can capture any other Gu if commanded (ch 38).
- **Longevity Gu:** Increases lifespan (ch 38).
- **Cultivation mechanics:** Rank 1 essence is green copper, Rank 2 is red iron, Rank 3 is white silver. One portion of a higher rank equals 10 of the lower (ch 26). Aperture walls upgrade from light barriers (initial stage) to water barriers (middle stage) to stone barriers (upper stage) (ch 33). Primeval stones are drained of their natural essence during cultivation, shrinking into white powder (ch 34). Gu without food can self-seal into hibernation, turning into rock fossils (ch 40).
- **Economy:** Primeval stones are greyish-white and duck egg sized (ch 23). Grass puppets cost 3 stones (ch 23). Moon orchid petals cost 1 stone for 10 pieces (ch 23). Green bamboo wine costs 2 stones per jar (ch 23). Intimate grass costs 2 stones per catty (ch 39). Blue Sea Cloud tea brick costs 5 stones (ch 39). Gambling rocks cost 5, 10, or 20 stones (ch 40). Academy weekly stipend is 3 stones, monitor bonus is 10 stones (ch 26).

## 6. Creatures and nature
- **Wolf packs:** A wolf tide strikes the village roughly every three years. The wolves' den is currently filling up. The pack contains 3 Thunder Crown Wolves, which are highly dangerous (ch 31).
- **Caravan beasts:** Include a 15-meter silkworm with black porcelain armor, brightly feathered ostriches, hairy mountain spiders, winged snakes, and cow-sized pack toads (ch 39). 
- **Secluded Blood Bats:** Wild creatures that attacked the merchant caravan en route (ch 39).

## 7. Events and conflicts
- **Moonblade Assessment:** Students throw moonblades at grass puppets. Most struggle. Fang Yuan intentionally misses once, then seamlessly throws two blades that decapitate the puppet, shocking the crowd (ch 25).
- **Gateway Extortions:** Fang Yuan blocks the academy gates to extort 1 primeval stone per student. He uses brutal but non-lethal close combat to knock out resistors. He successfully robs the class twice, earning ~56 stones each time (ch 27, 28, 30).
- **Hostel Confrontation:** Mo Yan and her servant Gao Wan try to intimidate Fang Yuan at his dormitory. Fang Yuan sits inside with the door open, baiting her. Afraid of breaking academy rules, she leaves Gao Wan to stand guard overnight in the cold (ch 32, 33).
- **Murder of Gao Wan:** Fang Yuan exits at midnight and fights Gao Wan. Outmatched in pure physical stamina, Fang Yuan breaks rules and uses middle-stage powered moonblades to sever Gao Wan's arms and decapitate him (ch 34, 35).
- **The Minced Corpse:** Fang Yuan chops Gao Wan's body into a bloody paste, puts it in a wooden box, and leaves it at the Mo family's secluded back door. Elder Mo Chen realizes it is both a peace offering (back door) and a threat to cause a scandal. He pays Fang Yuan 30 stones as a cover-up (ch 36, 37).
- **Rock Gambling:** Fang Yuan uses 60 stones to buy 6 purple gold fossils. He grinds them down with moonblades. The fifth rock yields a live Rank 2 Mudskin Toad, which he immediately sells to the shopkeeper for 500 stones. He takes the empty sixth rock home to claim he found his Liquor worm inside it (ch 40-43).

## 8. Mood and time
- **Season and Weather:** Late spring transitioning into summer (May) (ch 39). The weather fluctuates between bright sunny days with flower scents, chilly nights with a crescent moon, and light spring rain that falls like fine mist (ch 25, 33, 38).
- **Tone:** Bleak, ruthless, and deeply cynical. Fang Yuan views humanity and society as a system of "rules and regulations" to be manipulated. He operates as a demon walking in the light, exploiting loopholes and using extreme violence casually (ch 38). 

## 9. Recurring objects
- **Primeval stones:** The universal currency and cultivation fuel, kept in money pouches. 
- **Green bamboo wine:** Purchased constantly by Fang Yuan in jars to feed his Liquor worm.
- **Grass puppets:** Woven targets used by students to practice martial arts and moonblades.
- **Gu Master belts:** Belts with numbered metal plates denoting the wearer's rank (ch 31).
