# T-043c0b4b2b — essential world-warning fallback (S76)

## Ownership and integration contract
`CastWarningGate.new(Config.CastWarning, normalRenderer, warningRenderer)` exclusively owns a fresh CastClockGate, primary CastBridge and independent CastWarningReserve. Drive predict/accept/cancel/retirePose/tick/destroy through this outer owner only; never mutate the exposed inner states. Call tick even without messages. Inputs are canonical generation-qualified keys and validated accepted timelines; delivery/metadata mapping remains b3/b4c. All calls, renderers and disposers are synchronous and non-yielding.

The primary bridge's optional keyed unavailable callback runs **after** primary partial allocations are disposed. It observes valid capacity-refused acceptance, immediate renderer false/throw, delayed future-queue drain failure and later seek failure. Predictions, malformed/future/out-of-range/expired receipts and terminal replays do not start a warning. The clock queue itself is still bounded and refuses overflow; a not-yet-due receipt is not bypassed into an early warning. Callback exceptions increment `bridge.callbackFailures` without locking admission.

Reserve rendering uses the same proven reducer/registry but its own capacity and allocation budget. Frames expose key/action/time/immutable timeline/phase/progress and world-only `bind(slot, disposer)`, `isCurrent(ticket)`, `release(ticket)`. No pose, prediction, damage or contact command exists. Bind before enabling callbacks; seek existing geometry, never re-emit one-shots. A remaining active world hazard is NOT proof of a hit. Renderer false/throw tombstones cosmetics and disposes partial allocations. Renderer true must really mean the essential cue was handled; allocating before binding or silently claiming success cannot be repaired by the owner.

A reserve-owned key (including terminal) blocks a second normal start when primary capacity recovers. Explicit server rejection/cancellation removes both pools; a terminal suppresses a same-key fallback during clock advancement. Other due queued warnings still progress. Unknown/healthy-only cancellations stay in the primary terminal ledger rather than consuming the independent reserve. Local death/pose loss leaves accepted world warnings alive. The b4c adapter still must verify current character/generation before creating a pose, including delayed acceptance after respawn.

A failed predicted renderer retains only its bounded predicted reducer identity, with no resources. It must not masquerade as server rejection. A true acceptance gets a fresh `start` lease (not adoption of missing visuals) and may fall back. Failed accepted normal rendering stays tombstoned to prevent restarts. A real terminal after failed prediction still wins.

## Initial Config policy and telemetry
- Primary: 256 reducer entries, 16 allocations/cast, 1s prediction timeout, 10s max timeline, 12s terminal retention.
- Clock: 64 pending receipts, 0.5s future tolerance, 2s local wait deadline, 1s sample tolerance, correction limited to 0.25s per elapsed local second.
- Reserve: 64 reducer entries, 2 allocations/warning, same 10s lifetime and 12s terminal retention. Tombstones share each reducer's bound; this is a deliberate conservative memory limit, not guaranteed capacity for 64 simultaneous visible warnings.
- 10s lifetime + 0.5s future allowance fits 12s terminal retention and the existing client receipt contract. These are concrete initial cosmetic budgets, NOT device-profiled tuning or a change to authoritative damage timing.
- `fallbacks` counts eligible primary degradation attempts, not visible success. `reserve.offers/refusals`, each bridge's `renderFailures/resourceRefusals/refusals/callbackFailures`, and each resource pool's `cleanupFailures` remain separate. Clock `badSamples/expired/refusals` remain on the clock gate. A successful tick means valid clock processing, not guaranteed rendering. Both pools can saturate; existing warnings are never evicted for new arrivals.
