# R-012 — IP & licensing (S4, 2026-10-30)

Not legal advice. Goal: decide how close Robby can safely get to *Reverend Insanity* (RI) on Roblox.

## 1. Rights holder & licensing
- RI is by Gu Zhen Ren, serialized on Qidian (China Literature) 2012–2019; the English edition is licensed on Webnovel (2,334 chapters). [1][2]
- Who holds game rights, and on what terms, is not public. Adaptations need the rights holder's consent and usually payment. [3]
- In 2019 the novel was removed from Qidian during a Chinese regulatory campaign (violence, cruelty, individualism). It remains unfinished. [1][4]
- There is no official anime or game. The only licensed adaptation is a manhua by Ran Hui Culture; a 2025 fan manhua is unofficial. [4][5]
- → A license is very unlikely for a small Roblox team: the title is banned in its home market, its rights sit with a large publisher, and it has never licensed a game.

## 2. Roblox enforcement
- Roblox acts on DMCA notices from rights holders; it does not pre-screen. Content is often removed without warning. [6][7]
- Examples: Shinobi Life 2 was deleted after a VIZ Media strike; Anime Adventures and Anime Fighting Simulator X were shut down over My Hero Academia assets. [8][9]
- Roblox's Terms and Community Standards forbid using IP (names, characters, branding) you don't own or license. Others getting away with it is no protection. Roblox runs an official IP licensing program. [10][11][12]
- → One notice can delete the game and all its progress/revenue. The risk is existential, not cosmetic.

## 3. What "inspired by" allows (US law; Roblox is US-based)
- Idea–expression dichotomy (17 U.S.C. §102(b)): rules, systems and mechanics are not protected; expression is (art, code, characters, settings, text). [13][14]
- *Tetris Holdings v. Xio* (2012): copying the look of a game infringed even though its rules were free. [13][15]
- *Spry Fox v. LolApps* (2012): the core concept was free, but specific characters and setting were protected. [14]

### Safe (keep)
- Systems: living-creature abilities, feeding, taming, fusion recipes, combo moves, ranks with sub-stages, affinities, placed formations, clan-vs-outcast factions.
- Themes and tone: cunning, scarcity, ambition, survival.
- Folklore and genre: gu folklore, xianxia realms, sects, tribulations.

### Not safe (never)
- The novel's title in the game name, thumbnails, tags or ads.
- Any names: characters, clans, villages, mountains, regions, sects, named creatures, named moves.
- Plot, arcs, events, the protagonist's premise as written.
- The novel's coined terms (its specific names for the inner energy store, the rank essences, combo moves, the immortal realm).
- Text, quotes in-game, fan-wiki or manhua art.

### Grey (decide in planning, default = avoid)
- The word "Gu" itself: it is real folklore, but players will read it as RI. Recommendation: use our own term for the creatures.
- Saying "inspired by RI" in the description: helps discovery but invites attention from the rights holder. Recommendation: don't.
- Colour-coded rank energy: a generic idea, but don't reuse the novel's exact colour-metal sequence and names together.

## 4. Unknowns
- Exact owner of game rights (author vs. China Literature) — not public.
- How strict non-US rules (China, EU) would apply; Roblox enforcement is the practical gate.
