# R-011 — Source study: Reverend Insanity (S4, 2026-10-30)

Purpose: understand what makes the source work, so Robby can borrow **mechanics and feel** without copying **IP** (D-014).
Sources are listed at the bottom; [n] marks a source. Items from model memory are labelled "(unverified)".

## 1. Findings

### Gu (the core system)
- Gu are living creatures used as tools/abilities. Gu Masters power them with essence. [1][2]
- Ranked 1–9: ranks 1–5 mortal, 6–9 immortal. [1][3]
- Each Gu must be fed a specific food. Higher-rank Gu eat more but less often. [1]
- Getting Gu: subdue a wild Gu's will ("refinement"), or create one from materials or by fusing Gu with a recipe into a higher rank. [2][4]
- Masters group Gu into sets around a core Gu for battle efficiency. [1]
- Killer moves: several Gu activated as a fixed, synergistic sequence. Stronger than single Gu, but cost more essence and mental strain. Usually one "path"; multi-path moves are stronger/versatile but suffer friction. [5][6]
- Formations: Gu placed at a fixed location using environmental "dao marks"; vulnerable if core parts are destroyed. [5][7]
- Paths: power is split into elemental/thematic paths (strength, fire, etc.), tracked as dao marks. [5][7]

### Cultivation
- The aperture is an inner space storing primeval essence; it opens at 15 at an "Awakening Ceremony" = rank 1. [8][9]
- Each rank has 4 stages: initial, middle, upper, peak. [10]
- Aptitude grades A–D set aperture size, essence regen speed and the cultivation ceiling. [9]
- Essence is colour-coded by rank: green copper (1), red iron (2), white silver (3), yellow gold (4), purple crystal (5). [8][11]
- Rank 6 is the immortal threshold: the aperture becomes a "blessed land" producing immortal essence. Rank 9 = apex. [10][3]

### World & factions
- The world has five regions separated by regional walls. The southern region is huge and mountainous; clans are bloodline-based and build villages on mountain peaks. [12][13][14]
- The story opens in a clan village on a mountain, with rival villages nearby, built around an underground spirit spring. [15][16]
- Righteous path = clans, bloodline hierarchy, security. Demonic path = loners outside clan protection, using force/theft/ruthless competition. [17][18][19]
- The central region uses talent-based sects instead of clans; ten great sects dominate. [20][21]

### Tone, themes, reception
- Dark cultivation story; an amoral, ruthless protagonist reborn with 500 years of memories, pursuing immortality by cold logic. [22][23]
- Praised: long-term strategic plotting, "realistic" cruel world, subversion of the just-world trope. [22][24][25]
- Criticised: bloated length, pacing, coincidences, incompetent side characters, bleak/unrewarding tone, extreme villainy. [24][26]

## 2. Classification

### (a) Game-able mechanics (OK to use in an original form)
- **Living-creature abilities**: abilities are creatures you capture, tame, feed and level. Feeding = upkeep/resource sink.
- **Rank ladder 1–9 with 4 sub-stages** each → clear progression bar. Colour-coded energy per rank = readable UI and VFX.
- **Aptitude grade** → per-character stat for energy pool and regen (care: don't hard-lock players; let it be improvable).
- **Taming / capture**: subdue a wild creature's will (minigame or combat).
- **Fusion recipes**: combine 2 creatures + materials into a higher-rank one → crafting/discovery loop.
- **Loadout of N creatures around a core** → build-crafting.
- **Combos ("killer move" style)**: an ordered set of abilities becomes a stronger move with extra cost. Great action-RPG hook.
- **Paths/affinities** (strength, fire, wind…) with mixing penalties → build identity and specialisation.
- **Placed formations**: stationary traps/zones with a breakable core → PvE/PvP objectives.
- **Clan vs. outcast factions** → two faction choices with different perks (security vs. freedom/stealing).
- **Mountain-village start + awakening ceremony** as a tutorial beat (generic coming-of-age).

### (b) IP-specific — must NOT copy (D-014)
- All names: characters (incl. the protagonist), clans, villages, mountains, sects, regions, named Gu, named killer moves.
- The plot: rebirth-with-500-years premise as framed in the novel, specific arcs and events.
- Exact system names/wording where they are coined by the novel (e.g. its specific essence names, "blessed land", its named ten sects).
- Text, chapters, long quotes, fan-wiki art.
- Safer: use our own names for everything (rank energy colours with our own names, our own term for the creature/ability).

### (c) Generic / folklore (free to use)
- **Gu folklore**: historical Chinese belief — venomous creatures sealed in a jar devour each other; the survivor concentrates the poison, used for curses/wealth. Records date to oracle bones (2nd millennium BCE). The link to Miao people is considered by researchers to be a projection/stereotype → avoid attaching it to any real ethnic group. [27][28]
- **Xianxia conventions**: cultivation realms, sects as schools/powers, tribulations (lightning tests to advance), spirit-stone-style currency, inheritances. These are genre conventions, widely used. [29][30]

## 3. → Implications for Robby
- Core fantasy candidate: **"tame, feed, fuse and combo living abilities"** on a rank ladder — fits action-RPG + collection.
- Combos give combat depth comparable to Rell Seas move sets (to check in R-013).
- Readers love strategy and ruthlessness; a Roblox audience needs neutral wording for minors → keep the dark, cunning tone in world design (scarcity, rival clans, betrayal-free PvP choices) without gore/cruelty.
- The jar-fight origin can be a world-building/ritual mechanic (e.g. "crucible" fusion event) in original form.
- Avoid ethnic coding of gu-users (folklore note above).
